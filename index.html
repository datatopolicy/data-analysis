About the workshop

This is Phase 1 of the Data Analysis and Report Writing Project: a Training of Trainers that equips national teams to independently replicate and adapt this workshop model in their own countries, reducing long-term dependence on CDC Foundation or consultant facilitation.

Over the project's full arc, government agencies identify priority topics and data needs (Phase 2), participants explore and prepare their own data virtually (Phase 3), this in-person workshop builds their analysis into a written report (Phase 4), participants finalize and publish that report on a government platform such as a national public health bulletin (Phase 5), and teams set up the standard operating procedures to reproduce the report on a regular cycle (Phase 6). This week is where the technical and facilitation skills for all of that are built.

PhaseWhat happens
1. Training of TrainersThis workshop. Four country teams build the analytical, reporting and facilitation skills to run the project at home.
2. Topics, teams and data accessGovernment agencies confirm up to four priority topics, 2–3 participants per team, and begin the data request process.
3. Data explorationParticipants clean their own data, identify trends, and establish analysis objectives ahead of the in-person workshop.
4. Report writing workshopThe four-day, in-person format modeled this week: review key concepts, apply best practices in writing and visualization, present results.
5. Finalize and disseminateWith mentor support, participants finalize and publish their report on a government platform.
6. Reproduce regular reportsTeams set up the SOP that lets the next edition of the report be produced from a refreshed extract, on a monthly, quarterly or annual cycle.

Project objectives

  1. Train participants to access, clean, analyze and communicate surveillance and mortality data.
  2. Train participants to access and use census data to calculate rates.
  3. Establish best practices and document SOPs for accessing surveillance and mortality data.
  4. Introduce best practices in data visualization — tables, figures and maps.
  1. Emphasize equity — gender, socioeconomic status and location — in policy proposals and communication products.
  2. Develop templates for reporting on priority diseases and conditions.
  3. Establish a monitoring and evaluation framework tracking competency gains, output quality and post-training data use.
  4. Build a cadre of in-country trainers who can sustain and scale this work.
