Session 5.109:00–09:30 · 30 minutesPlenary

Day 4 debrief

The final daily debrief, and a direct bridge into this morning's presentations: confirming every team has a complete draft and their presentation content selected before the room moves into presenting.

Learning objectives

  1. Confirm every team has a complete draft report and a selected set of presentation highlights.
  2. Understand the format and expectations for this morning's presentations.

Detailed content

Structured recall (10 min)

A shorter version of the usual three questions, given the day's tight schedule: is your draft complete, and are your presentation highlights selected from yesterday's final thirty minutes.

Presentation format and expectations (20 min)

Explain the presentation format: each country team presents for roughly fifteen minutes using the group report presentation template, covering their objective, headline results with the equity disaggregation, and their top recommendations, followed by five minutes of questions from the room. State the assessment lens the room should use while watching each other present: is the objective clear, are results kept separate from interpretation, does at least one figure or table meet the standards from Wednesday, and are the recommendations specific enough to act on.

Exercise 5.1: Final presentation check

Country teams · 15 minutes within the session

  1. Confirm your slide deck follows the presentation template and stays within the time limit when read through once.
  2. Assign who on your team presents which section.
  3. Do a single full read-through as a team before the session ends.

Facilitator notes

Timing
Do not let this debrief run long — the morning's presentation schedule is fixed and tight.
Common pitfall
A team plans to present every table and figure from their report rather than the highlights selected yesterday. Redirect firmly to the three to four selected highlights.

Key takeaways and assessment checks

  • Present the selected highlights, not the entire report.
  1. What four things should the room be listening for during each presentation?

Session 5.209:30–10:30 · 60 minutesPlenary presentations

Group report presentations, part one

The first two country teams present their reports to the full room, using the standard presentation template, followed by structured questions from peers and facilitators.

Learning objectives

  1. Present a complete analysis clearly within a fixed time limit.
  2. Respond to questions about methods, denominators, and interpretation under real conditions.
  3. Practice giving specific, constructive feedback to a peer team's presentation.

Detailed content

Format (full session)

Two country teams present, roughly fifteen minutes each plus five minutes of questions, totaling the hour. Facilitators moderate questions to keep them specific and constructive — a question about which denominator was used or how a completeness gap was handled is more useful at this stage than a general comment on style.

Exercise 5.2: Present and question

Two country teams presenting, full room participating · 60 minutes

  1. Team presents for approximately fifteen minutes using the standard template.
  2. Room asks specific questions for five minutes: about the objective, the denominator, the disaggregation, or the recommendations.
  3. Facilitator notes one strength and one specific suggestion for each team, to be shared privately afterward rather than during the plenary session.

Facilitator notes

Timing
Enforce the time limit visibly — a fifteen-minute presentation that runs to twenty-five eats into the second team's slot and the following break.
Common pitfall
Questions drift into a debate about the underlying health topic rather than the analysis and reporting choices this week has focused on. Redirect gently back to methods, denominators, and communication.
Real-world edge case
A presenting team is asked a question they cannot answer on the spot. Model the response taught back in the week's training-methodology thread: say so plainly, note it as a follow-up item, and move on rather than guessing.

Required datasets, tools and resources

Each presenting team's completed slide deckFrom Thursday's Session 4.6.

Key takeaways and assessment checks

  • A clear, time-limited presentation communicates more than a comprehensive but rushed one.
  1. What is the appropriate response when a presenting team cannot answer a question on the spot?

Session 5.311:00–13:00 · 120 minutesPlenary presentations

Group report presentations, part two

The remaining two country teams present, followed by a whole-room synthesis discussion identifying the strongest practices seen across all four presentations.

Learning objectives

  1. Present a complete analysis clearly within a fixed time limit.
  2. Identify, across all four presentations, the strongest examples of the week's standards in practice.

Detailed content

Format (100 min)

The remaining two teams present in the same fifteen-minutes-plus-five-minutes-questions format as this morning.

Cross-country synthesis (20 min)

With all four presentations now delivered, run a short synthesis discussion: which team's table best exemplified the standalone-table standard from Wednesday; which recommendation was most specific and actionable; which team handled a data quality limitation most honestly. This closing discussion consolidates the week's standards using the room's own real work as examples, rather than abstract ones.

Exercise 5.3: Present, question, and synthesize

Two country teams presenting, then full-room discussion · 120 minutes

  1. Remaining two teams present and take questions, following this morning's format.
  2. As a full room, identify the strongest table, the strongest recommendation, and the most honest limitation across all four presentations from the day.
  3. Facilitators note these examples for use in future TOT cycles.

Facilitator notes

Timing
Protect the synthesis discussion — it is easy to let presentations run long and lose this closing block, but it is where the week's standards get reinforced through the room's own examples rather than facilitator assertion.
Common pitfall
The synthesis discussion turns into general praise rather than specific identification of what worked and why. Push for specificity: which exact table, which exact sentence.

Required datasets, tools and resources

Each presenting team's completed slide deckFrom Thursday's Session 4.6.

Key takeaways and assessment checks

  • The week's standards are most memorable when identified in the room's own real examples.
  1. Name one specific table or recommendation from today's presentations that met the week's standards, and say why.

Session 5.414:00–14:30 · 30 minutesPlenary

Training methodologies: reflections for national rollout

The week's Training of Trainers thread comes to a head: participants reflect explicitly on the demonstrate-guide-release method and the facilitation choices they experienced all week, now as the trainers who will use them at home.

Learning objectives

  1. Identify which facilitation techniques from this week they intend to use in their own training.
  2. Recognize the specific data-access and technical challenges from this week that their own training should anticipate.

Detailed content

What to notice, now that you have lived it (15 min)

Walk back through the week's structure explicitly as a facilitation case study: the fixed daily debrief structure (used every day since Tuesday), the demonstrate-guide-release sequence (introduced Session 2.2, used in every technical session since), and the short, focused technical sessions paired with substantial applied team time. Ask participants which of these they had not consciously noticed happening until this session named it.

Anticipating your own room's version of this week's challenges (15 min)

Revisit the recurring data-access challenges logged since Session 1.3 and ask each participant to consider: will your own trainees face the same barriers, and how would you structure day one of your own training to surface them early, the way this week's Session 1.3 did.

Exercise 5.4: Two techniques you will use

Individual, then shared in pairs · 15 minutes

  1. Name two specific facilitation techniques from this week you intend to use when you train colleagues at home.
  2. Name one challenge from this week's parking-lot chart you expect your own trainees to face, and one way you would surface it on day one of your own training.
  3. Share both with a partner from a different country team.

Facilitator notes

Timing
Keep this reflective and brief — the concrete planning work follows immediately in Session 5.5.
Common pitfall
Participants name generic good facilitation habits rather than the specific techniques actually used this week. Redirect to the named techniques: the three-question debrief, demonstrate-guide-release, and the diagnose-before-fixing habit from Sessions 1.5 and 2.6.

  • The week's own structure is itself a facilitation model worth naming explicitly, not just experiencing.
  1. Name two specific techniques from this week you will use when training colleagues at home.

Session 5.514:30–16:00 · 90 minutesCountry teams

Country rollout planning

This block was not explicitly labeled in the source schedule; it is shown here as dedicated rollout-planning time so the week closes with a concrete, actionable output for each country. Confirm or replace with your finalized agenda.

Learning objectives

  1. Produce a country-specific data analysis project plan, the project's required post-workshop deliverable.
  2. Name the specific data access actions from Session 1.3 that must be resolved before the next cycle.
  3. Draft recommendations for revising this week's workshop materials, the second required post-workshop deliverable.

Detailed content

What the plan must contain (20 min)

Walk the country-specific data analysis project plan template, which covers: the priority topics and teams for the next cycle; the specific data access actions carried forward from Session 1.3's challenge log, each with an owner and date; the target dates for finalizing and publishing this week's report, per the project's Phase 5; and how this country will approach reproducing the report on a regular cycle, per Phase 6.

Drafting the plan (45 min)

Each team drafts its plan directly from artifacts already produced this week: the Session 1.3 access pathway and challenge log, the Session 1.6 week plan, and this week's actual report as the first edition the reproducibility plan will need to match.

Feedback on workshop materials (25 min)

Each team also completes the recommendations for revising workshop materials form — the second required post-workshop deliverable — noting which sessions, exercises or materials worked well and which need revision before the next country's Training of Trainers cycle.

Exercise 5.5: Draft your rollout plan

Country teams · 90 minutes

  1. List your priority topics and confirmed team members for the next cycle.
  2. Pull forward every unresolved item from your Session 1.3 challenge log, each with an owner and a date.
  3. Set target dates for finalizing and publishing this week's report.
  4. Note, at least briefly, how this report will be reproduced next quarter or next year.
  5. Complete the workshop materials feedback form as a team.

Facilitator notes

Timing
Ninety minutes is enough for a genuine first draft, not a polished final plan — say so explicitly, and note that mentors will follow up on the plan after the workshop.
Common pitfall
Teams write aspirational dates disconnected from the realistic timelines documented in Monday's Session 1.3 pathway worksheet. Push back on any date that contradicts the team's own earlier documentation.
Real-world edge case
A team's most experienced member may not be available to lead their own country's next training. Note this directly in the plan rather than assuming it will resolve itself.

Session 5.616:00–16:30 · 30 minutesPlenary

Workshop evaluation and wrap-up

A short closing session: formal evaluation of the week, an honest look back at the confidence markers set on Monday, and confirmation of what happens next for each country team.

Learning objectives

  1. Complete the workshop evaluation form.
  2. Reflect honestly on whether Monday's stated confidence levels and concerns have changed.

Detailed content

Evaluation (15 min)

Distribute the workshop evaluation form, covering content, pacing, facilitation, and materials, and give participants time to complete it before leaving the room. This is the primary input, alongside the workshop materials feedback form from Session 5.5, for revising the curriculum ahead of the next country's Training of Trainers cycle.

Returning to Monday's confidence markers (10 min)

Read back the one-word confidence markers each team gave in Session 1.1's introductions. Ask, honestly, whether the word has changed and why — resist the pull to declare universal success; an honest "still uncertain about X" is more useful to the mentoring relationship that follows than a polished but false sense of closure.

What happens next (5 min)

Confirm, plainly, the immediate next steps for every team: submitting both post-workshop deliverables, the timeline toward finalizing and publishing this week's report under Phase 5, and how mentor support will be arranged in the weeks following the workshop.

Exercise 5.6: Close the loop

Plenary · 10 minutes within the session

  1. Each team states, in one sentence, whether their Monday confidence word has changed and to what.
  2. Each team confirms they know the submission deadline for both post-workshop deliverables.

Facilitator notes

Tone
Keep this warm and brief; it follows a demanding week and should close it rather than open a new discussion.
Non-negotiable
Do not end the workshop without every participant having submitted the evaluation form and every team confirming their post-workshop deliverable deadlines out loud.

Required datasets, tools and resources

Key takeaways and assessment checks

  • Honest closure, including unresolved concerns, serves the following mentorship better than false reassurance.
  • Every team leaves knowing exactly what is due, and when.
  1. What are the two required post-workshop deliverables, and who is responsible for submitting them?
